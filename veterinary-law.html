Common questions

Veterinary Practice Owner FAQs.

I just received an LOI from NVA / VCA / Thrive / Pathway. What should I do first?

Don't sign anything, and don't respond with a counter-number yet. An LOI is non-binding on most major terms, but it sets the tone and timeline for the entire transaction. The first step is a confidential review with independent counsel — we'll walk through the proposed structure, identify which terms are standard and which are negotiable, and help you understand the real value of the offer before you respond.

How is a veterinary practice sale to a PE platform different from selling to another vet?

PE platform deals are structurally different from a traditional doctor-to-doctor sale. They typically include an earnout (a portion of the price contingent on future performance), an equity rollover (you retain a stake in the larger platform), and a multi-year employment agreement with production targets and restrictive covenants. Each of these components needs to be negotiated separately — and each carries real financial and lifestyle implications beyond the headline purchase price.

What is an earnout, and why do most sellers end up with less than they expected?

An earnout ties part of your sale price to the practice's future performance — often revenue or EBITDA targets over 1-3 years post-close. The risk: once you've sold, you typically no longer have full control over staffing, pricing, hours, or marketing decisions that drive those numbers. We negotiate earnout metrics, definitions, and protections so the targets are realistic and within your influence — not dependent on decisions made by the new owner.

Do I need a veterinary-specific attorney, or can my general business attorney handle this?

A general business attorney can review a contract, but veterinary PE transactions follow patterns specific to this consolidation wave — the same buyers, the same deal structures, the same negotiation points, repeated across hundreds of transactions nationally. We've seen the actual paper that NVA, VCA, Thrive, and Pathway use, and we know which clauses move and which don't. That pattern recognition is the value of vertical-specific counsel.

I'm not selling yet — I'm just curious what my practice might be worth in a few years. Is it too early to talk to an attorney?

It's actually the ideal time. Many of the things that affect your eventual sale value and deal terms — entity structure, real estate ownership, associate agreements, partnership terms — are far easier to put in order years before a sale than during a 45-day diligence period. An early consultation costs you nothing and can meaningfully improve your position when the time comes.

How long does a veterinary practice sale typically take from LOI to closing?

Most PE platform transactions move from signed LOI to closing in 60-120 days, depending on practice complexity, real estate involvement, and how quickly diligence items are produced. Multi-location transactions take longer. We move efficiently — our role is to keep your deal on schedule without sacrificing the negotiation points that matter.

My CPA or broker recommended I talk to you. What happens on the first call?

The first call is a free, confidential conversation — no obligation. We'll talk through where you are in the process (early-stage curiosity, an LOI in hand, or mid-negotiation), what documents you've already received, and what your priorities are. From there we'll outline next steps and, if it makes sense, schedule a full engagement.
