Healthcare Practice Sell-Side Representation — Private Equity Transactions.

We represent the practice owner — not the platform. Independent counsel ensuring sellers in med spa, optometry, and veterinary practices understand exactly what they're signing before they sign it.

Schedule a Deal Consultation

Private equity has become the dominant buyer across veterinary, optometry, and medical spa practices — the same consolidation pattern that has reshaped healthcare practice ownership over the last decade. NVA, VCA, Thrive, and Pathway in veterinary. Total Vision, Acuity, Keplr, MyEyeDoctor, and EyeCare Partners in optometry. PE-backed MSO platforms and aesthetics funds in med spa.

Every one of these platforms is represented by sophisticated M&A counsel that has executed dozens — sometimes hundreds — of these transactions. The seller, almost always, has not. That imbalance is the entire reason independent sell-side representation exists, and it's the only thing we do in the PE context: represent the selling practice owner, never the buyer, never the platform, and never the broker (though we work closely and efficiently with brokers on the seller's behalf).

A PE acquisition of a healthcare practice is rarely a simple asset purchase. It typically combines several distinct components — purchase price mechanics, an earnout tied to future performance, an equity rollover into the larger platform, and a multi-year employment or management agreement governing the seller's role post-close. Each component requires separate negotiation, and each has real financial consequences beyond the headline number.

Deal types covered

Every Structure in a Modern Platform Transaction.

Whether this is your first sale or your platform's fifth add-on, we've negotiated this structure before.

  • Platform Builds

    The initial acquisition that establishes a new PE platform in a vertical — typically the largest, most heavily negotiated transaction in a roll-up's lifecycle.

  • Add-On Acquisitions

    Subsequent practice acquisitions into an existing platform — often on more standardized terms, but still requiring independent review of seller-specific provisions.

  • Recapitalizations

    When a platform's PE sponsor sells to a new sponsor ("second bite"), practice owners with rolled equity face a new round of decisions about their stake.

  • Management Buyouts

    Representation for management teams or practice leadership acquiring ownership stakes — including financing structure and governance negotiation.

  • Earnout Structuring

    Negotiating the metrics, definitions, time horizon, and protections around any portion of your purchase price contingent on future performance.

  • Equity Rollover Agreements

    Understanding what you're actually receiving when proceeds are rolled into platform equity — governance rights, liquidity timeline, and dilution protections.

  • Post-Close Employment Agreements

    Often more contentious than the purchase price: production requirements, non-competes, scheduling autonomy, and management authority after the sale.

  • Diligence Coordination

    Managing the document production and disclosure process so diligence stays on schedule without exposing you to unnecessary risk.

Common questions

PE Transaction FAQs.

Who do you represent in a PE transaction?

Exclusively the selling practice owner. We do not represent PE platforms, their lenders, or brokers on the buy side. This singular focus is what allows us to negotiate aggressively on your behalf without any conflicting incentives.

What's the difference between an earnout and an equity rollover?

An earnout is additional purchase price contingent on the practice hitting specific future performance targets — it's still "your" money, but you only receive it if the targets are met (and you may have limited control over the factors that determine that). An equity rollover means a portion of your sale proceeds is reinvested into equity of the larger platform, giving you ongoing ownership (and risk) in the combined company rather than cash at closing.

How much of my purchase price is typically "real" cash at closing?

This varies significantly by deal and platform, but it's common for a meaningful portion of total consideration to be contingent (earnout) or illiquid (equity rollover) rather than cash at closing. Understanding this breakdown — and negotiating the terms of the non-cash portions — is one of the most important parts of our review.

I'm a PE operating partner — why would I want the seller to have their own attorney?

Sellers represented by competent independent counsel tend to close faster and generate fewer post-close disputes, because they understand what they agreed to. An unrepresented or poorly-represented seller is more likely to raise issues late in diligence, push back unpredictably, or contest terms after closing. Independent counsel on the seller's side is, in practice, a risk-reduction tool for the platform too.

Do you charge a percentage of the deal, or a flat/hourly fee?

Fee structures depend on the scope and complexity of the engagement — we'll discuss this transparently during your initial consultation, before any engagement begins. There's no cost or obligation for the first conversation.
